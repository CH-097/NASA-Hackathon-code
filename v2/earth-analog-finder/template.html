    HOW THE SCORES ARE MADE

    1. Retrieve Earth data. NASA POWER satellite climatology for each site (temperature, day–night range, precipitation, humidity, sunlight, wind; aerosol depth where available). Bulk gridded data via NASA Earthdata and the earthaccess Python library (NetCDF-4 with xarray).
    2. Retrieve Moon / Mars data. NASA PDS: LRO Diviner for lunar temperature and illumination; MRO Mars Climate Sounder and MGS TES for Mars. OPUS (pds-imaging.jpl.nasa.gov/search) is the easiest search front end.
    3. Score similarity. Each factor is put on a common scale. Similarity = 100% at an exact match, falling linearly to 0% once the gap reaches the factor's spread. The overall score is the weighted average of the factors you switch on.
    4. Visualize. Scores per site and per map cell are shown on the heat map, the globe and the site summaries.

    Best file formats: NetCDF-4 for gridded climate, GeoTIFF for rasters such as DEMs. Avoid HDF4 and shapefiles where possible.
    Limits: roughness is an estimate index; the heat map between named sites is interpolated; Earth cannot reproduce reduced gravity, vacuum or a thin CO₂ atmosphere, or the radiation environment.
